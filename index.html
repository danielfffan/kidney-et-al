02 — About

I'm a PhD student in the Wallace H. Coulter Department of Biomedical Engineering, a joint program between Georgia Tech and Emory University, where I work on computational pathology methods for kidney diseases.

My research focuses on quantitative and deep learning–based analysis of whole-slide histology images — segmenting and characterizing glomeruli, tubules, peritubular capillaries, and interstitial tissue to better understand how structural change relates to disease progression in kidney diseases. I also pair these image-derived features with multi-scale molecular data, such as spatial omics and bulk transcriptomics.

Outside the lab, I'm generally found behind a camera, out on a tennis court, or nursing a beer over conversations that have nothing to do with my thesis.

I recently found myself deep down a Jazz rabbit hole and started teaching myself piano — wish me luck. :)

03 — Research

Computational tools for renal histopathology.

I.

Computational Pathology

Deep learning models for whole-slide image segmentation, detection, and quantification of renal tissue structures at scale.

II.

Renal Morphometry

Structural and morphological analysis of glomeruli, tubules, and interstitium across stages of chronic kidney disease.

III.

Multimodal Modeling

Integrating histology, clinical variables, and molecular data to model and predict disease trajectories.

IV.

Open Tools & Datasets

Building open-source annotation pipelines and benchmark datasets for the renal pathology research community.
